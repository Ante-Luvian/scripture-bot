# ScriptureBot changelog

What's new, newest first. When the bot starts after an update, she posts any
entries a server hasn't seen yet to that server's changelog channel
(`!changelogsetup #channel`); anyone can read recent ones with `!changelog`.

Format: one `## YYYY-MM-DD · Title` section per notable change (new commands,
new features, fixes users would notice), with `- ` bullets written for users.
Once an entry has been announced, don't change its heading — the heading is
how the bot remembers what it has already posted.

Write every bullet for someone who has never seen the code or this project's
history (see CLAUDE.md): one change per bullet; say in plain words what's new
or what was wrong; give an example command to try; explain any term a casual
reader wouldn't know.

## 2026-10-18 · Jewish learning of the day
- ✡️ **New: `!parsha`, this week's Torah portion.** The Five Books of Moses are read through in synagogue every year, a portion (*parashah*) each Shabbat. `!parsha` tells you this week's: what it's about, its seven readings (each with the command to read it), and the passage from the Prophets read with it, the *Haftarah*. `!parsha 12-25` gives another week's.
- 📖 **New: `!dafyomi`, today's page of the Talmud.** In the *Daf Yomi* ("page of the day") cycle, Jews around the world study the same double-sided page of the Talmud each day, finishing all 2,711 pages in about seven and a half years. `!dafyomi` shows you today's page.
- 📅 **New: `!learning`, all of the day's learning.** Besides the Torah portion and Daf Yomi there are many daily cycles: a Mishnah a day, Maimonides' code of law, a chapter of the Bible, Jewish law, the Tanya and more. `!learning` lists today's, each with the command to read it. Server admins can have it posted every morning with `!learningsetup channel #channel`.
## 2026-10-18 · Fixes: "I Kings", "I John" and long Jewish references
- 🔧 **Books written with Roman numerals now open the right book.** `!verse I Kings 9` showed *2 Kings* 9, and `!verse I John 1:1` showed *3 John*: the bot didn't understand Roman numerals and guessed the nearest name. Now `I`, `II`, `III` and `IV` work like `1`, `2`, `3` and `4`, and the old Catholic names `III Kings` and `IV Kings` (`3 Kings`, `4 Kings`) open 1 and 2 Kings. A misspelt numbered book, like `1 Jhon`, also keeps its number.
- 🔧 **Passages of Jewish texts that cross a chapter now open.** References like `!verse Mishnah Oholot 10:7-11:1` or `!verse Shulchan Arukh, Orach Chayim 277:4-278:1` said "Unknown book"; now they're looked up in Sefaria's library like any other.
## 2026-10-18 · Sikh scripture: the Dasam Granth and Bhai Gurdas
- ☬ **New: the Dasam Granth.** After the Guru Granth Sahib, the Dasam Granth is the second great book of Sikh scripture: the writings of Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Guru, beginning with the *Jaap Sahib*, a hymn of praise Sikhs recite every morning. Read it by page and line, in English: `!verse Dasam Granth 1`, `!verse Dasam Granth 1:5-8`.
- 📜 **New: the Vaaran of Bhai Gurdas.** Forty ballads by Bhai Gurdas, the scribe who wrote out the first copy of the Guru Granth Sahib; Guru Arjan called them the key to understanding it. Cite them by ballad (*vaar*) and stanza (*pauri*): `!verse Bhai Gurdas 1:1`, or a whole vaar with `!verse Vaaran 1`.
- 📖 **A second translation of the Guru Granth Sahib.** `!verse Guru Granth Sahib 1:1` still gives Sant Singh Khalsa's translation; add `manmohan` for Manmohan Singh's (`!verse Guru Granth Sahib 1:1 manmohan`), or both names to compare them. `!help sikh` shows it all.
## 2026-10-18 · The Bhagavad Gita: more translations, and its great commentaries
- 🕉️ **Five more English translations of the Bhagavad Gita.** The Gita is the Hindu scripture in which the god Krishna teaches the warrior Arjuna on the eve of battle. `!verse Gita 2:47` still gives Shri Purohit Swami's translation; put a name after it for another: `sivananda`, `gambirananda`, `adidevananda`, `sankaranarayan` or `prabhupada` (the *Bhagavad-gītā As It Is* of the Hare Krishna movement). Name several to compare them: `!verse Gita 2:47 purohit sivananda prabhupada`.
- 📜 **The classic commentaries, verse by verse.** For over a thousand years Hindu teachers have explained the Gita line by line, and their commentaries founded whole schools of thought. Now you can read them in English: `!verse Gita 2:47 shankara` (Shankara, the great teacher of non-dualism), `ramanuja` (Ramanuja, of devotional "qualified non-dualism"), `abhinavagupta` (the Kashmiri philosopher), and `purport` for Prabhupada's explanations. `!help gita` lists them all.
- 🔧 **A stray word is gone from Gita 2:48.** The verse began "Revised:", an editor's note left in the source.
## 2026-10-18 · The Pali Canon
- ☸️ **New: the whole Pali Canon, the earliest Buddhist scriptures.** These are the Buddha's teachings as the oldest school of Buddhism, the Theravāda, preserved them in the ancient Pali language, thousands of discourses (*suttas*) in all. They come from SuttaCentral in Bhikkhu Sujato's modern English translation, and you cite them the way Buddhists do: `!verse MN 1` (the Middle Discourses), `!verse DN 16` (the Long Discourses: the Buddha's last days), `!verse SN 56.11` (the Linked Discourses: his first sermon, on the Four Noble Truths), `!verse AN 3.65` (the Numbered Discourses: his advice to the Kālāmas on what to believe), plus the Udāna, Itivuttaka, and the verses of the first Buddhist monks and nuns (`!verse Thig 1.1`). Long suttas turn pages with ◀ ▶, and `!verse MN 1:3` shows just one section. `!help buddhist` has the full guide.
- 📖 **Sujato's Dhammapada and Sutta Nipāta too.** `!verse Dhammapada 183` still gives the bot's older translation; add `sujato` for the new one: `!verse Dhp 183 sujato`.
## 2026-10-17 · The Quran in 52 English translations
- ☪️ **The Quran now comes in M.A.S. Abdel Haleem's translation.** Abdel Haleem's 2004 translation for Oxford World's Classics is the one most used by scholars and universities today: accurate and in clear modern English. `!verse Quran 2:255` (the Throne Verse) now shows it. Until now the bot used Marmaduke Pickthall's 1930 translation, whose old-fashioned "thee and thou" English matched the King James Bible.
- 📚 **Every other well-known English translation is there too, 52 in all.** Put a translator's name after the reference: `!verse Quran 2:255 arberry` for A. J. Arberry's literary *The Koran Interpreted*, `studyquran` for *The Study Quran*, the major scholarly translation of 2015, `asad` for Muhammad Asad, `sahih` for Saheeh International, `pickthall` for Pickthall, and many more, including the first English translations by George Sale (1734) and J. M. Rodwell (1861). `!help quran translations` lists them all.
- ⚖️ **Compare translations side by side.** Name several: `!verse Quran 1:1 haleem arberry asad` shows the same verse in each, one after another.
## 2026-10-17 · Hadith: the Forty Hadith Qudsi
- ☪️ **New: the Forty Hadith Qudsi.** A *hadith qudsi* is a saying of God reported in the words of the Prophet Muhammad, a special class of hadith, between the Quran and the ordinary sayings. This classic collection of forty of them joins the Bukhari, Muslim, Nawawi and other collections the bot already has. Try `!verse Qudsi 1`.
- 🔧 **The last hadith of two collections now work.** Sunan an-Nasa'i was listed as running to 5,761 and Muwatta Malik to 1,852; the real last numbers are 5,758 and 1,858, and `!help` and `!books` now say so. The last book of Muwatta Malik also couldn't be reached by some lookups; now it can.

## 2026-10-17 · The rest of Sefaria's library
- ✡️ **`!verse` now reaches every book in Sefaria's library that has an English translation.** Sefaria is the free online library of Jewish texts. The bot already looked up the Talmud, the Mishnah, the Zohar, the Midrash and a few others there; now it can fetch anything else Sefaria has, using Sefaria's own name for the book. That includes the classic Bible commentaries (`!verse Rashi on Genesis 1:1`), Judah Halevi's *Kuzari*, a medieval dialogue defending Judaism (`!verse Kuzari 1:1`), *The Path of the Just*, a beloved guide to living a holy life (`!verse Mesillat Yesharim 1`), the *Shulchan Arukh*, the standard code of Jewish law (`!verse Shulchan Arukh, Orach Chayim 1:1`), the stories collected from the Talmud in *Ein Yaakov*, and Hasidic teachings like Rebbe Nachman's *Likutei Moharan*. If a book is on Sefaria only in Hebrew or Aramaic, the bot says there's no English translation rather than showing it untranslated. `!help jewish` lists examples.
## 2026-10-17 · Icons: easier to find
- 🔎 **`!icon list` now shows every other name each icon answers to.** Many icons are known by several names, and the bot already understood them: `!icon smolensk` finds the Hodegetria (the Smolensk icon), `!icon easter` the Resurrection, `!icon christmas` the Nativity. The list didn't say so, which made it look like icons were missing. Now every entry shows its other names too.
- 👑 **`!icon mary` shows all the icons of Mary.** A word that fits several icons used to open whichever came first. `!icon mary` opened the Nativity of the Theotokos. Now you get a list of every icon it fits (for Mary, the Theotokos of Vladimir, Kazan, Tikhvin, the Hodegetria, the Annunciation, the Dormition and more) to choose from.

## 2026-10-17 · The Divine Liturgy
- ☦️ **New: `!divineliturgy`.** The Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom is the Orthodox Church's Eucharist, the service most Orthodox churches celebrate every Sunday, little changed for well over a thousand years. Now you can read the whole of it in the bot, a page at a time, in an English translation of 1866: the prayers and litanies, the hymns, and, in italics, what the priest and deacon do as they say them. ◀ ▶ turn the pages, and a menu takes you to any part: the Great Litany, the Little Entrance, the Gospel, the Cherubic Hymn and Great Entrance, the Creed, the Anaphora (the great prayer of offering), the Lord's Prayer, Holy Communion and the Dismissal. Or ask for a part directly: `!divineliturgy creed`.

## 2026-10-17 · Icons
- ☦️ **New: `!icon`.** Icons are the holy images of the Orthodox Church: painted on wood in an ancient, unchanging style, and read rather than just looked at, since every colour, gesture and figure means something. `!icon` shows you one, with an explanation of what it shows and its story. There are the most famous icons, like Andrei Rublev's *Holy Trinity* (the three angels who visited Abraham), the *Theotokos of Vladimir*, the 6th-century *Christ Pantocrator* of Mount Sinai and the *Ladder of Divine Ascent*, and the icons of the twelve **Great Feasts** of the Church year and of Pascha (Easter).
- 📅 **The feast's icon on its day.** On a Great Feast (Christmas, Theophany, the Annunciation, Palm Sunday, Pascha, the Ascension, Pentecost, the Transfiguration, the Dormition…) `!icon` shows that feast's icon. Pascha and the feasts that move with it follow the Orthodox date of Easter. Other days it shows a famous icon. Ask for any: `!icon vladimir`, `!icon transfiguration`, or `!icon list`.

## 2026-10-17 · The Orthodox hymns of the day
- ☦️ **New: `!troparion`.** Every day the Orthodox Church remembers its saints and feasts in song. Each has a short hymn of its own, the *troparion*, and usually a second, the *kontakion*, sung at the day's services. Each is sung in one of eight *tones*, the eight melodic modes of Byzantine chant. `!troparion` gives you today's, each with its tone, as the Orthodox Church in America publishes them. The line breaks show where each sung phrase ends. Ask for another day with `!troparion 12-25`.
- ⏰ **For server admins: `!troparionsetup`.** Have the bot post the day's troparia in a channel every morning: `!troparionsetup channel #orthodox` (with `time 06:00` in UTC, `test`, `off`).

## 2026-10-17 · Orthodox prayers
- ☦️ **New: prayers of the Orthodox Church.** `!prayer` now has the prayers Eastern Orthodox Christians say every day, each in English and in its original **Greek**:
  - **The Jesus Prayer**, "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner": the "prayer of the heart" that Orthodox monks repeat through the day on a prayer rope. `!prayer jesus prayer`
  - **The Trisagion**, "Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us" ("thrice holy"), which begins nearly every Orthodox service. `!prayer trisagion`
  - **O Heavenly King**, the prayer to the Holy Spirit that opens Orthodox prayers.
  - **It Is Truly Meet** (*Axion Estin*), the hymn to the Theotokos (Mary, "the God-bearer") sung at every Divine Liturgy.
  - **Rejoice, O Virgin Theotokos**, the Orthodox form of the Hail Mary.
  - **The Prayer of Saint Ephrem**, "O Lord and Master of my life", the great prayer of Lent.
  - **The Nicene Creed as the Orthodox say it**, without the phrase "and the Son" that the Western Church later added. `!prayer orthodox creed`
- `!prayer list` now shows the Orthodox prayers in their own section.

## 2026-10-15 · Traditional prayers
- 🙏 **New: traditional prayers by name.** `!prayer` followed by a prayer's name now gives you that prayer, in English and, where it has one, in Latin. There are eighteen, including the **Prayer to Saint Michael** (`!prayer st michael`), the **Memorare**, the **Anima Christi** ("Soul of Christ, sanctify me"), the **Act of Contrition**, the **Angel of God** prayer to your guardian angel, the **Morning Offering**, the **Sub tuum praesidium** (the oldest known prayer to Mary), the **Divine Praises**, the **Prayer to Saint Joseph**, the **Prayer of Saint Francis**, **Come, Holy Spirit**, **Eternal Rest** for the dead, and **grace before and after meals**. Each comes with a line about where it comes from.
- 🎤 **Sung where they're also chants.** The Memorare, the Anima Christi, the Sub tuum, the Salve Regina, the Our Father, the Hail Mary and Come, Holy Spirit come with their Gregorian chant sung in Latin.
- `!prayer list` shows them all; `!prayer` on its own still gives a random prayer from the 1662 Book of Common Prayer.

## 2026-10-15 · Psalm scores
- 🎼 **The chanted psalms now have their music too.** Under a psalm from `!psalm 110 chant` there's a **🎼 Score** button, like the one on the chants: the psalm written out in square chant notation with its Latin words, showing just how the psalm tone fits the words. Most of each line is sung on one note (the "reciting" note), and the melody moves only at the end of each half-line, on the stressed syllables. The red ✱ marks the pause in the middle of each verse. Long psalms come in pages with ◀ ▶.

## 2026-10-15 · The Rosary
- 📿 **New: `!rosary`.** The Rosary is the best-known Catholic prayer devotion: five *decades*, each an Our Father, ten Hail Marys and a Glory Be, prayed while thinking on one of the *mysteries*, events in the lives of Jesus and Mary like the Annunciation, the Crucifixion or the Resurrection. `!rosary` walks you through it a page at a time: the opening prayers, each decade with its mystery and the Bible passage that tells it, then the closing *Hail, Holy Queen*. Press ▶ Next to move on. The *Ave Maria* comes sung in Latin to pray along to.
- 📅 **The day's mysteries.** Each day of the week has its set: the Joyful (Monday and Saturday), the Sorrowful (Tuesday and Friday), the Glorious (Wednesday and Sunday) and the Luminous (Thursday). `!rosary` picks today's, or ask for one: `!rosary glorious`.

## 2026-10-14 · Chant scores
- 🎼 **New: see the music of the Latin chants.** Gregorian chant has its own notation: four lines instead of five, and square notes called *neumes*, sometimes joined into shapes that show several notes sung on one syllable. Every Latin chant now has a **🎼 Score** button under it that shows its music written that way, the way chant books have printed it for centuries, with the Latin words under the notes. Follow along while you listen to ▶️ the organ or 🎤 the singing.
- 📖 **Long chants come in pages.** Long pieces like the *Exsultet*, the Creed or the *Dies irae* are split into pages, with ◀ ▶ buttons to turn them. Try `!hymn Salve Regina` and press 🎼 Score.

## 2026-10-14 · The psalms, chanted in Latin
- 🎤 **New: every psalm chanted in Latin.** For centuries monks and nuns have sung all 150 psalms to the *psalm tones*: eight simple Gregorian melodies where most of each line is sung on one note and the melody moves only at the end of each half-line. Now the bot sings them that way, with the same synthesised choir as `!chant`. Ask for any psalm by its usual number: `!psalm 110 chant` (or `!chant psalm 110`).
- 📜 **Latin and English together.** Each psalm comes with its Latin, as the old Breviary (the book of the daily prayers) prints it, and the **Douay-Rheims** English, the classic Catholic Bible translation, so you can follow along. Each psalm ends with the *Gloria Patri* ("Glory be to the Father…"), as it does when sung in church.
- ▶️ **On the organ too.** Press **Organ** under a chanted psalm to hear the same melody played on the organ, or ask for it directly: `!psalm 23 chant organ`.
- 🔢 **Numbering.** The Latin Bible numbers most psalms one lower than English Bibles, so the bot shows both: `!psalm 23 chant` gives Psalm 23, "Vulgate 22", *Dominus regit me*. Psalm 119, the longest at 176 verses, is sung in its 22 sections as monasteries sing it: `!psalm 119 chant` for the first, `!psalm 119 chant beth` for the second, and so on.

## 2026-10-14 · The Angelus
- 🔔 **New: `!angelus`.** The Angelus is a short traditional prayer said three times a day: morning, noon and evening, when church bells ring for it. It recalls the angel Gabriel's message to Mary and the Incarnation. Three short verse-and-response lines, each followed by a Hail Mary, then a closing prayer. `!angelus` gives it in English and in Latin, with the *Ave Maria* chant **sung** attached so you can listen along.
- 🌸 **In Eastertide it changes by itself.** From Holy Saturday until the day before Trinity Sunday, the Church says the joyful *Regina Cæli* ("O Queen of heaven, rejoice! Alleluia") instead. `!angelus` gives that in those weeks, with the *Regina caeli* chant sung.
- ⏰ **For server admins: `!angelussetup`.** Have the bot post it in a channel every day: `!angelussetup channel #prayer` posts it at noon (UTC), and `!angelussetup time 06:00 12:00 18:00` posts it at all three traditional hours. `!angelussetup test` posts it now, `!angelussetup off` stops it.
- The words come from *A Manual of Prayers for the Use of the Catholic Laity* (1889), the official prayer book of the American Catholic bishops.

## 2026-10-14 · Chant, sung
- 🎤 **New: hear the Latin chants sung.** Until now the bot's Gregorian chants (the ancient Latin hymns, sequences, antiphons and the chants of the Mass) could only be played on an organ. Now every one of them can also be heard *sung*, with its Latin words, by a small synthesised choir of chanters. The voice is a simple computer voice, not a recording of people. It sings the Latin the way the Church does, the Italian way (*caelis* as "chelis", *regnum* as "reñum"), one steady note at a time, in the echo of a church.
- 🎤 **A Sung button.** Open any chant with `!hymn` (for example `!hymn Pater noster`) and press **🎤 Sung** under it.
- 🎶 **New command: `!chant`.** It sings a chant straight away, without the words to scroll past. `!chant Ave Maria`, `!chant Salve Regina`, `!chant Dies irae`, or `!chant` on its own for a random one. Also works as `/chant`. `!hymnlist chant` lists every chant there is.

  - **J. M. ## 2026-10-14 · English translations of the Latin chants
- 🇬🇧 **New: an English button on the Latin chants.** Many of the Latin hymns and chants now have a **🇬🇧 English** button under them that shows a published English translation beside the Latin. Only real, published translations are used; chants without one stay in Latin.
- ⛪ **The Mass in the Prayer Book's English.** Every setting of the Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus and Agnus Dei shows the Book of Common Prayer's English: "Lord, have mercy upon us", "Glory be to God on high", "I believe in one God", "Holy, holy, holy, Lord God of hosts", "O Lamb of God, that takest away the sins of the world". ## 2026-10-14 · Hail, Gladdening Light
- 🕯️ **The *Phos Hilaron* is now John Keble's translation, *Hail, gladdening Light, of His pure glory poured* (1834)**, the best-loved English version, in place of Robert Bridges's *O Gladsome Light*. It comes with the tune Sir John Stainer wrote for it in 1875, *Sebaste*. Try `!hymn Hail gladdening light` or `!hymn Phos Hilaron`.

## 2026-10-14 · The chants of the Mass, and the Requiem
- ⛪ **Added: the sung parts of the Mass, in every setting.** These are the parts that stay the same at every Mass: the Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus and Agnus Dei, and the *Ite, missa est* that ends it. The chant books give many melodies for each, numbered I to XVIII (Mass VIII, for example, is the well-known *Missa de Angelis*), and all of them are here. Try `!hymn Credo III`, `!hymn Kyrie VIII` or `!hymn Credo in unum Deum` to choose among all seven Credos. `!hymnlist chant mass ordinary` lists them.
- 🕯️ **Added: the chants of the Requiem**, the Mass for the dead: the Introit *Requiem aeternam* ("Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord"), the Gradual, the Tract *Absolve, Domine*, the Offertory *Domine Jesu Christe*, the Communion *Lux aeterna*, and the responsories *Libera me* and *Subvenite*. Try `!hymn Requiem aeternam`. (The *Dies irae* was already here.)

## 2026-10-14 · O Gladsome Light (the Phos Hilaron)
- 🕯️ **Added: *O Gladsome Light*, the *Phos Hilaron*.** It's one of the oldest Christian hymns still sung, a Greek hymn for lamp-lighting at evening prayer, already old by the 300s. This is Robert Bridges's English translation of 1899, with the tune it's most often sung to: Louis Bourgeois's *Nunc dimittis* from the Genevan Psalter of 1551, in Claude Goudimel's harmony, played on the organ. Try `!hymn O Gladsome Light` or `!hymn Phos Hilaron`.

## 2026-10-14 · The Pater noster
- 🙏 **Added: the *Pater noster* (the Lord's Prayer) in Gregorian chant.** It had been asked for and was missing. The chant collection files it with the prayers of the Mass, not with the hymns, so it was left out when the Latin hymns were added. It's the version sung at Mass, from the *Graduale Romanum*, with the priest's introduction (*Praeceptis salutaribus moniti… audemus dicere*, "Taught by our Saviour's command… we dare to say"). Try `!hymn Pater noster`, or `!midi Pater noster` for just the chant.

## 2026-10-14 · One story, many traditions
- 📚 **New: `!story`, the same story as the world's traditions tell it, side by side.** `!story flood` puts Noah's ark next to Uta-Napishtim's boat in the Epic of Gilgamesh, Deucalion and Pyrrha in Greek myth, Ovid's Jupiter drowning the world, Noah in the Quran, and the Maya Popol Vuh's flood that destroys the first people, made of wood.
